The only honest methodology is this: **map the business first, then evaluate the technology.**

At Clear-Path, we call this "Process First. AI Second." It's not a marketing tagline — it's the methodology. When you map your workflows before you evaluate AI, you get two things: a clear picture of where AI can actually help, and a documented foundation that makes your business better whether AI is the answer or not.

The 5-Question Self-Test

Not sure if your business is even a candidate for AI adoption? Start here. Answer these honestly:

**1. Do you have tasks that repeat the same way every time?**

AI thrives on repetition. If a task requires judgment calls, relationship nuance, or situational creativity every time — that's human work. If it follows a consistent pattern — intake forms, appointment confirmations, follow-up sequences, invoice generation — that's AI territory.

**2. Do you spend more than 5 hours per week on admin that doesn't grow revenue?**

Five hours a week is 260 hours a year — more than six full work weeks. If that time is going to scheduling, data entry, report generation, or email follow-up, it's worth asking whether AI could reclaim any of it.

**3. Could you write step-by-step instructions for your most tedious task?**

This is one of the most important questions in AI readiness. If you can't document a task clearly enough that a new employee could follow it, an AI system definitely can't follow it either. Documentability is a prerequisite for automation.

**4. Do you have digital records of your key business data?**

AI tools work with data. If your client history lives in your memory, your job estimates are handwritten, or your scheduling exists only in a paper calendar — you have a data infrastructure problem that needs solving before AI can help.

**5. Are you spending money on problems that better processes could solve for free?**

This one stings a little, but it matters. Sometimes the bottleneck isn't that you need AI — it's that you need a standard operating procedure, a better handoff, or a clearer checklist. The right diagnosis isn't always "add technology."

If you answered yes to three or more of those questions, you're likely a strong candidate for an AI readiness assessment. If you answered no across the board, your next step might be process work — not AI implementation.

What a Real AI Assessment Actually Looks Like

Here's what rigorous AI consulting for small businesses looks like in practice — not a sales call, not a demo, but an honest evaluation:

**Step 1 — Process Map.** We document your actual workflows using structured process mapping. Not how you think things happen. How they actually happen, step by step, from trigger to output. This usually surfaces surprises.

**Step 2 — SOP Development.** From the process map, we draft Standard Operating Procedures for your core business functions. These become the foundation for any AI implementation — and they're valuable whether AI follows or not.

**Step 3 — AI Eligibility Scoring.** Each identified process gets scored on five criteria on a 1–5 scale: Repetitiveness, Data Availability, Error Cost, Time Investment, and Technical Feasibility. A composite score above a defined threshold is what we call "AI-eligible."

**Step 4 — ROI Calculation.** For eligible processes, we run actual numbers. Current hours × current labor cost, projected time savings, realistic tool costs, implementation time, and break-even timeline. No projections without assumptions on the table.

**Step 5 — Clear Recommendation.** Every assessment ends with one of four outputs: **Go** (strong AI fit, positive ROI, proceed to implementation), **Go with Caveats** (AI fits but with specific conditions or prerequisites), **Not Yet** (process work needed first; reassess in 3–6 months), or **No** (AI is not the right tool for this problem).

That last option — "No" — is one most AI vendors will never give you. We will. Because an honest "no" builds more trust than an enthusiastic yes that leads to a failed implementation.

Those artifacts make your business **easier to run, easier to delegate, and easier to scale** — independent of any technology decision.

The assessment pays for itself either way.
