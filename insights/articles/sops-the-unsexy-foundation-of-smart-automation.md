## Nobody gets excited about SOPs

Let's be honest. When a business owner hears "Standard Operating Procedures," their eyes glaze over. It sounds like corporate bureaucracy — binders full of policies that no one reads.

But here's the truth: **SOPs are the single most valuable asset a small business can create.** And they're the prerequisite for any meaningful automation.

## What an SOP actually is

An SOP is simply a written answer to: "How do we do this?"

Not how we *should* do it in theory. How we *actually* do it, step by step, every time. It's the difference between "we handle customer complaints" and:

1. Customer complaint received via phone, email, or in-person
2. Log complaint in shared tracker within 1 hour
3. Acknowledge to customer within 4 business hours
4. Assign to responsible team member
5. Resolve within 48 hours
6. Follow up with customer to confirm resolution
7. Log resolution and any process changes needed

**That's an SOP.** Clear, specific, repeatable.

## Why SOPs matter for automation

Every automation tool — AI or otherwise — needs instructions. It needs to know:

- What triggers the action?
- What are the steps?
- What are the exceptions?
- What does "done" look like?

If your team can't answer those questions for a process, no tool can automate it. You'll end up with an expensive system that handles the easy 60% and breaks on everything else.

## The SOP-first approach

When we work with clients, SOPs come before any technology discussion:

1. **Identify your core processes** — the 10-15 workflows that keep your business running
2. **Document each one** — have the person who actually does the work write it down
3. **Test it** — can someone unfamiliar follow the SOP and get the right result?
4. **Refine** — close the gaps, clarify the ambiguities
5. **Then automate** — now you know exactly what the tool needs to do

## The hidden benefit

SOPs don't just prepare you for automation. They immediately improve your business:

- **New hires ramp up faster** — they have a playbook
- **Quality becomes consistent** — it's not dependent on one person's memory
- **You can actually take a vacation** — because the knowledge isn't locked in your head

> The business that documents its processes is the business that can grow, automate, and adapt. The one that doesn't is permanently dependent on its founder's memory.

## Start small

You don't need to document everything at once. Pick your three most repeated processes. Write them down this week. That's your foundation.
