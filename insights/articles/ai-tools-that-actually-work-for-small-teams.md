## The enterprise trap

Most AI tools are designed for companies with 500+ employees, dedicated IT teams, and six-figure software budgets. When a 12-person company tries to use them, the result is predictable: too complex, too expensive, too much overhead.

But there are categories where AI genuinely helps small teams — if you pick the right tools and implement them correctly.

## Where AI delivers for small businesses

### 1. Scheduling and calendar management

AI scheduling tools eliminate the back-and-forth of booking meetings. For service businesses, this alone can save hours per week.

**What works:** Tools that integrate with your existing calendar, send automatic reminders, and handle rescheduling without human intervention.

### 2. Document drafting and templates

AI can generate first drafts of proposals, emails, reports, and standard documents. You still edit and approve — but the blank-page problem disappears.

**What works:** Tools that learn your tone and style over time, and that integrate with your document workflow (not standalone apps you have to copy-paste from).

### 3. Data entry and extraction

If your team spends time manually entering data from invoices, forms, or emails into spreadsheets or CRMs, AI can handle most of that.

**What works:** OCR + AI extraction tools that connect directly to your existing systems. The key is integration — a tool that creates *more* manual steps isn't saving you anything.

### 4. Customer communication triage

AI can sort, prioritize, and draft initial responses to incoming emails or messages. It won't replace your personal touch, but it can handle the first 80%.

**What works:** Tools that flag urgent items, auto-categorize requests, and draft responses you can review before sending.

## What doesn't work (yet)

- **AI that requires constant supervision** — if you're spending more time checking the AI's work than doing it yourself, it's not ready
- **Tools that don't integrate** — standalone AI apps that require copy-pasting between systems add friction, not efficiency
- **"AI-powered" features bolted onto bad software** — a bad CRM with AI is still a bad CRM

## The implementation rule

For any AI tool, apply this test:

> Will this save my team at least 3 hours per week after the first 30 days?

If the answer isn't a confident yes — based on your process map and baseline numbers — wait. The tool will still be there when you're ready.
