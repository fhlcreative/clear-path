## "Will AI save me money?"

It's the first question every business owner asks. And it's completely reasonable. But it's also premature.

The better question is: **"What is this process costing me right now?"**

## You can't calculate ROI without a baseline

If you don't know how much time, money, and effort a process currently consumes, you have no way to measure whether a new tool improves it. This is where most AI sales pitches fall apart — they promise savings against a number you've never measured.

## The three-number baseline

For any process you're considering automating or improving, you need three numbers:

1. **Time per occurrence** — How long does this take each time it happens?
2. **Frequency** — How often does it happen per week/month?
3. **Loaded cost** — What's the fully-loaded hourly cost of the person doing it?

**Time × Frequency × Cost = Your baseline.**

### Example

Your office manager spends 45 minutes per day manually entering invoice data.

- Time: 0.75 hours
- Frequency: 5x per week = 20x per month
- Loaded cost: $28/hour

**Monthly cost: 0.75 × 20 × $28 = $420/month**

Now you have a real number. If an AI tool costs $99/month and eliminates 80% of that work, your net savings are roughly $237/month. That's a clear, defensible ROI.

## When the math doesn't work

Sometimes it doesn't. And that's valuable information too.

If a process costs you $150/month in labor and the AI tool costs $200/month, the answer is obvious: don't buy it. But you'd never know that without measuring first.

> An honest ROI analysis protects you from bad investments just as much as it reveals good ones.

## The hidden costs people forget

Beyond the subscription price, factor in:

- **Setup and migration time** — someone has to configure the tool
- **Training** — your team needs to learn it
- **Maintenance** — tools need updates, troubleshooting, and oversight
- **Opportunity cost** — time spent on implementation is time not spent on revenue

## Start with the baseline

Before you evaluate any tool — AI or otherwise — measure what you're currently spending. The number might surprise you in either direction. And either way, you'll make a better decision.
