## It's not a flowchart on a wall

When most people hear "process map," they picture a giant flowchart with dozens of boxes and arrows. Something an engineer would build. Something that lives in a binder no one opens.

That's not what we're talking about.

## A process map is a story

A good process map tells the story of how work moves through your business. It answers:

- **Who** does what?
- **When** does it happen?
- **What triggers** the next step?
- **Where** do things get stuck?

It doesn't need special software. It doesn't need to be pretty. It needs to be *accurate*.

## A real example

Let's say you run a landscaping company. A new client calls in. What happens next?

1. Receptionist takes the call, writes the name on a sticky note
2. Sticky note goes on the office manager's desk
3. Office manager calls back (sometimes that day, sometimes two days later)
4. They schedule an estimate
5. Estimator goes out, writes notes on a clipboard
6. Notes get entered into a spreadsheet (eventually)
7. Quote gets emailed to the client

**That's seven steps with at least three failure points.** The sticky note gets lost. The callback is delayed. The clipboard notes are illegible.

## Why this matters for AI

You can't evaluate AI tools until you see the process clearly. Maybe step 1-3 could be replaced by an online booking form. Maybe steps 5-7 could be streamlined with a mobile estimating app.

But none of that is "AI" — it's just better process design. And that's the point.

> The map shows you what to fix. The fix might be AI, might be a checklist, might be a conversation with your team.

## How to build one

1. **Pick one workflow** — your most common or most painful
2. **Walk through it step by step** with the people who actually do the work
3. **Write down every step**, including the informal ones ("then I usually text Mike")
4. **Circle the pain points** — delays, errors, redundancies
5. **Ask: what would ideal look like?**

That's your process map. Simple, honest, and incredibly useful.
